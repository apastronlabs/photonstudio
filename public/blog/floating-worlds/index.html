Creating believable AI video commercials requires solving two classic pain points: hardware hallucinations (where buttons and wheels warp randomly) and character drift (where the actor's face changes every cut).

In this tutorial, you will master the Zero-Gravity Commercial Workflow. By providing video diffusion models with orthographic multi-angle reference sheets, we lock every hardware contour and facial feature into place before directing dynamic camera motions.

1. How It Was Made: The 5-Step Pipeline

Rather than relying on one lucky generation, this commercial follows a structured 5-stage production process:

01
The Physics Hook

A tactile scroll wheel action disables gravity in sync. Everyday items, then room walls, then cars float into the sky.

02
Product Orthographics

Lock exact dimensions (128.2mm), MagSpeed wheel knurling, thumb wing, and haptic pad on a clean studio sheet.

03
Persona Casting

Establish a photorealistic human anchor: 22yo graphic designer, wolf cut, natural skin texture, ARRI 50mm lighting.

04
Turnaround Sheet

Multi-panel front, profile, back, and macro tiles lock costume and facial morphology across dynamic camera turns.

05
Seedance 2.5 Cuts

Pass both sheets as dual references into Seedance 2.5 with locked lenses (16mm to 100mm macro) and foley sound rules.

2. Product Reference Sheet (Logitech MX Master 4)

The biggest challenge in commercial AI is hardware fidelity. Standard diffusion models often invent extra buttons or smooth out metal knurling. The fix is generating an orthographic multi-panel product sheet first.

Reference Image 2: Multi-panel orthographic sheet (top, front, side profile, macros, hand holding mouse). Studio Sheet

  • check_circle Knurling & Materials: Explicitly mention solid brushed-silver knurled metal, matte graphite soft-touch body, and chrome ring accents.

3. Character Casting: The Human Anchor

Before asking a video engine to animate someone, establish their facial features in high resolution. Avoid generic keywords like "gorgeous model" or "hyperrealistic", which result in plastic, unconvincing AI faces. Instead, specify natural imperfections, tangible wardrobe details, and specific camera lighting.

Reference Image 1 (Casting): 22-year-old creative designer with natural skin texture and morning window sunlight. Persona Anchor
  • check_circle Micro-Anchors: A small mole on the left cheek, small silver nose stud, messy wolf cut with wispy bangs, and thin silver index ring guarantee consistency.
  • check_circle Texture Defense: Demand "visible pores, fine peach fuzz, no retouching, no smoothing" to banish waxy diffusion artifacts.
  • check_circle Lens Heritage: Specify ARRI Alexa with a 50mm lens and gentle morning sidelight for cinema-grade contrast.

In a dynamic commercial, the camera circles the actor from multiple directions (16mm low angle, 35mm overhead, eye-level profile). A single portrait causes the model to guess what the back of her head or her outfit looks like. A Turnaround Sheet provides full 360° spatial coverage.

5. 8-Shot Video Choreography (Seedance 2.5)

Now assemble both references into Seedance 2.5. Rather than giving a vague description, break the 15-second commercial into 8 precise director cuts with locked lens values, shutter rules, and physics triggers.

Shot 1: The Discovery

0.0 – 2.5s 16mm Wide Angle

Low desk angle in a sunny Manhattan loft. As she scrolls lazily, her ceramic coffee mug lifts into mid-air. She smiles in playful surprise and whispers: "Wait... what?"

Camera: Handheld slow push-in diagonally across desk • Horizon locked.

Shot 2: Tactile Focus

2.5 – 4.0s 100mm Macro

Extreme close-up from the front at desk level. Her index finger rolls smoothly across the knurled metal MagSpeed wheel, turning in sync.

Camera: Low front push-in • Wheel turns WITH finger, never under a still hand.

Shot 3: Gravity Switches Off (The Hook)

4.0 – 6.5s 16mm Dynamic

She scrolls deliberately. Every object in the loft peels off the floor and floats up—plants, vinyl records, lamps, cushions. The walls and giant window lift off whole, revealing a sunny NYC crosswalk around her desk.

Camera: Energetic push-in, quick tilt up following flying objects, back down.

Shot 4: The Crosswalk From Above

6.5 – 8.0s 35mm High Angle

Overhead view from a 4th-floor window looking down. Her desk stands centered in the zebra crosswalk as pedestrian traffic steps around her in shock.

Camera: Slow downward drift • Yellow taxi paused before crosswalk.

Shot 5: Diving Into The Free Spin

8.0 – 10.5s 15° Yaw Dive

Camera flies at high speed toward the mouse, yaws 15° to the right, and dives directly into the center of the scroll wheel as she flicks it into infinite free-spin mode.

Camera: High-speed continuous dive • Horizon locked, zero camera roll.

Shot 6: The Entire Street Lifts Off

10.5 – 12.0s 18mm Low Angle

Looking up from the asphalt. Solid slabs of street lift into the sky along with real yellow NYC taxis and black sedans, turning slowly with heavy physical weight.

Camera: Low handheld push-in • Floating autumn leaves and street dust.

Shot 7: Center of the Storm

12.0 – 13.5s 24mm Eye Level

Frontal push-in. She sits laughing at her desk in the street center as shadows of soaring cars sweep across her face, wind lifting her hair upward.

Camera: Fast direct push-in • Her hand remains firmly on the mouse.

Shot 8: The Haptic Stop

13.5 – 15.0s 100mm Crash Zoom

Rapid crash zoom onto the spinning metal wheel. Her index finger drops straight down onto the knurling, halting the wheel instantly with a tactile haptic thud.

Camera: Crash zoom to complete freeze • Finger presses and holds.

GLOBAL STYLE
15-second 9:16 commercial for the Logitech MX Master 4 mouse. Live-action look shot on 35mm film (Kodak Vision3), vintage spherical lenses, 180-degree shutter, natural motion blur. Real materials, real cars, real weight. NOT CGI, NOT plastic, NO beauty retouch. Energetic young camera: handheld, fast push-ins, crash zooms, varied angles and lenses. The horizon stays level: the camera never rolls or flips. Hard cuts. No on-screen text. NO MUSIC, only sound effects.

REFERENCES
The FIRST reference image is the girl: exact face, hair and clothes in every shot: petite, about 22, wispy fringe, messy bun, charcoal hoodie, nose stud, pearl earrings, silver ring on right index finger, nude nails.
The SECOND reference image is the mouse: Logitech MX Master 4 in graphite, exact shape: low, wide sculpted body, one solid brushed-metal knurled scroll wheel between the two buttons, a USB-C port on the front face under the wheel, metal thumb wheel on the left side, haptic thumb pad, green LED. Same shape and size in every shot.

RULES
- Real scrolling: only her index finger moves. The fingertip rolls over the metal wheel and the wheel turns WITH the finger. The wheel never turns under a still finger.
- The world reacts to her scrolling like gravity switching off: every object lifts off separately and flies up on its own, tumbling at its own speed. Objects stay whole, no cracking, no shattering.
- Her desk always holds a slim black monitor, a white keyboard, a ceramic coffee mug, a dark felt desk mat and the mouse. She and her desk never float.
- Light and wind on her always match her surroundings.

SHOT 1 (0.0-2.5s) - She notices
- Lens: 16mm, very low camera at the desk surface at the near left corner, looking diagonally across the desk at her. The mouse under her hand is the main object in the lower left foreground, sharp. Her face is the second focus. The keyboard is in the middle; the coffee mug stands FURTHER AWAY, next to the monitor on the far side of the desk, small in frame, never in the foreground. Handheld, slow push-in toward the mouse.
- Location: a cozy high-floor Manhattan loft in the morning. Behind and beside her a HUGE floor-to-ceiling window that fills most of the background, with a wide view of Manhattan: rooftops, water towers, the Empire State Building. Warm sun through the window, hard light on one side of her face.
- Action: she scrolls lazily, bored, eyes on the monitor. With her scroll, the coffee mug on the far side of the desk lifts a few centimeters into the air and hangs there, coffee level. Her eyes move to it. She scrolls once more, carefully: the mug rises higher, in sync. A half-smile of amused surprise spreads across her face, one corner of her mouth lifting in a playful smirk, and she says softly, with a little disbelieving laugh: "Wait... what?" Still smirking, she looks down at the mouse.

SHOT 2 (2.5-4.0s) - One slow scroll, from the front
- Lens: 100mm macro. The camera looks at the mouse from the FRONT, low at desk level: the front face with the USB-C port, the metal wheel in the center above it, her fingertip on top of the wheel. Slow push-in.
- Action: ONE slow, long, continuous scroll: her fingertip starts on the front top of the wheel and slowly rolls back over it, away from the camera toward her palm, the knurled metal turning together with the fingertip. No button clicks.

SHOT 3 (4.0-6.5s) - Gravity switches off (HOOK)
- Lens: 16mm, same low angle as shot 1. Handheld, energetic push-in, then a quick tilt up following the flying objects and back down to her.
- Action: she scrolls on purpose, grinning. Gravity in the room turns off. Object by object, everything around her lifts off the floor and walls and flies upward, each piece separately, tumbling and spinning at its own speed with real weight: the plants and their pots, soil floating out of them, the floor lamp, books opening mid-air, vinyl records sliding out of their sleeves, the record player, framed prints, cushions, the rug peeling off the floor, a chair. Then the walls and the big window, frame and glass intact, lift off and fly upward in large whole pieces, never dissolving or fading.
- Revealed behind: a real sunny Manhattan street around her: a zebra crosswalk under her desk, dark asphalt, brick buildings with fire escapes, autumn trees with yellow leaves, glass towers, clear blue sky.
- As the window flies up and away with the wall, the warm window light on her is replaced by crisp autumn daylight from the side, a breeze moves her fringe. She looks around, amazed, laughs.

SHOT 4 (6.5-8.0s) - The crosswalk from above
- Lens: 35mm, high angle from a fourth-floor window, looking steeply down, slow drift.
- A straight street with a zebra crosswalk across it from sidewalk to sidewalk, its white stripes parallel to the street. Her desk stands in the middle of the crosswalk, she sits scrolling, long shadow over the stripes. Pedestrians cross the street on the crosswalk from one sidewalk to the other, stepping over the stripes. A man with a coffee steps around her desk and looks back, confused. A yellow taxi waits before the crosswalk.

SHOT 5 (8.0-10.5s) - Diving into the free spin
- Starts: a straight frontal medium shot of her hand on the mouse on the desk mat, camera level, the sunny street blurred behind.
- Camera move: the camera flies fast toward the mouse. While flying, it yaws about 15 degrees to the RIGHT, so it arrives at an angle and dives straight into the CENTER of the scroll wheel, ending in an extreme macro where the knurled metal fills the frame. One continuous, smooth, fast move. The horizon stays level, no roll.
- Action: as the camera flies in, her index finger makes ONE strong, fast stroke and lifts completely off the wheel. The wheel spins freely on its own, faster and faster, the knurling blurring, sunlight flashing on the metal edges.

SHOT 6 (10.5-12.0s) - The street lifts off
- Lens: 18mm, very low angle from the asphalt, looking up at a red-brick building with fire escapes, an autumn tree and a glass tower against the blue sky. Handheld, slight push-in.
- Action: the whole street lifts. The asphalt itself rises in big whole slabs together with the cars on it. Two real yellow NYC taxis and a black sedan lift off and rise diagonally upward past the building, turning slowly with real weight, their wheels hanging. Clouds of street dust, newspapers and autumn leaves fly up around them. The brick buildings grow taller, stretching upward floor by floor. People stop and look up.

SHOT 7 (12.0-13.5s) - She in the center
- Lens: 24mm, eye level, frontal, she sits in the center at her desk on the street, orange traffic cones around. Fast push-in toward her.
- Action: on both sides and behind her, taxis and cars lift off the asphalt and float up, leaves and newspapers rising. Wind lifts her fringe and loose strands upward, shadows of rising cars sweep over her face. She looks up, delighted, laughing, her hand on the mouse.

SHOT 8 (13.5-15s) - The stop
- Lens: 100mm macro, low three-quarter angle on the wheel. A fast crash zoom in, then the camera freezes completely.
- Action: the metal wheel is spinning fast by itself. Her index finger hovers above it, then lowers straight down onto it. The wheel stops dead under the fingertip, the mouse gives a tiny haptic jolt. She only presses and holds, no scrolling.

LIGHTING AND GRADE (very important)
A graded feature-film look, like a scanned film negative, NOT a phone video: no HDR, no over-sharpening, no clipped highlights anywhere, no blown-out sky or windows. Highlights roll off softly, blacks slightly lifted and milky, gentle halation around bright edges, visible film grain, soft lens with a light vignette. Colors look painted and even: rich but controlled, smooth tones across surfaces, not glossy.
Shots 1-2: warm morning sun through the big window, the window view stays visible, not white.
Shots 3-8: clear autumn day, sun low from the side, long soft-edged shadows, deep teal-blue sky, warm saturated yellow taxis and golden trees, brown brick, grey asphalt with warm sunlit patches. NOT orange, NOT golden hour glow.

SOUND (no music)
Quiet room tone. A soft rolling ratchet sound of the wheel whenever she scrolls, no button clicks. In shot 1 she says softly, amused and surprised, with a smirk in her voice and a tiny breathy laugh: "Wait... what?" (only these two words). Whooshes of objects flying up. City ambience, footsteps, wind. A sharp flick and a rising metallic whir on the free spin. Deep rumble of the lifting street. At the stop: total silence and one deep haptic "thunk". Heavy thuds as the cars land. A soft rolling wheel sound on the packshot, then quiet room tone and silence.

6. Foley Sound & Film Emulation

Two subtle choices give this commercial its grounded, premium feel:

  • volume_up Foley Over Music: Banning background music makes the video hit harder. The soundscape relies on mechanical tactile cues: the rolling ratchet of the wheel, the rising metallic whir of free spin, and the heavy physical thud of landing cars.
  • palette 35mm Film Grade (Kodak Vision3): Soft highlight roll-off, lifted milky blacks, and gentle halation eliminate the tell-tale hyper-glossy look of standard AI renders.
